Expression Evaluator

Break expressions into terms, evaluate step by step

Key Rule
Terms are parts separated by '+'. Convert all subtractions to addition of inverses first. Then evaluate each term (products/quotients inside a term), and finally add all terms together.

How to Use This Simulation

What To Do
1Choose an expression from the presets or use the slider to cycle through problems from the textbook.
2Look at the colored term boxes — each box is one term of the expression. Notice how products (like 2×6) stay together as a single term.
3Read the steps below: first each term's value is computed, then all terms are added together.
4Try the Quiz mode — guess the number of terms before seeing the answer!
Try This
Example: In 39 – 2×6 + 11, the terms are NOT 39, –2, 6, 11. The terms are: 39, –2×6, 11. The "2×6" is one term because there's no + sign splitting it!
Tricky: In 6×3 – 4×8×5, terms are 6×3 (=18) and –4×8×5 (=–160). Sum = 18 + (–160) = –142.
Key Concept
The '+' sign separates terms. Subtraction is just adding a negative. Products and quotients within a term are computed first. Then all term values are added. This is the correct order of operations!
