Sense of Scale

How big are large numbers, really?

What you discovered

How to Use This Simulation

What To Do
1Pick a scenario using the tabs at the top: Journey, Buses, Weight, Counting, or Facts.
2Drag the sliders to change values (speed, number of vehicles, weight, etc.). The result updates instantly.
3Watch the progress bars — they show how your result compares to real-world benchmarks (Moon distance, city populations, etc.).
4Read the discovery text at the bottom to understand what the numbers mean in context.
Try This
Journey: Set speed to 100 km/day and years to 10. You'll travel 3,65,000 km — almost reaching the Moon (3,84,400 km)! Now try 1000 km/day — can you reach the Sun in 100 years?
Buses: Set 50 people per bus and 1,00,000 buses. That's 50 lakh people — but Mumbai has 1.24 crore! You'd need about 2.5 lakh buses.
Weight: Set 5 grams per item and 1,00,000 items. That's 500 kg — no person can lift that! This is why 1 lakh sheets of paper is impossibly heavy.
Counting: Set 10,00,000 items at 1 per second. It takes 11.6 days! You can't count 1 million coins in a single day.
Key Concept
Large numbers feel different depending on context. 1 lakh rice varieties is a LOT to taste (274 years!), but 1 lakh people fit in one cricket stadium. To understand large numbers, always compare them to something familiar — a building height, a lifetime, a bus capacity.
